Sleep · Week 1–3
Why newborn sleep cycles differ from adult ones
What looks like a broken sleep schedule is actually one of the most important neurological processes of early life.
M
Dr. Mara Kleijn, Developmental Paediatrics
5 min read
Pediatrics, 2023
In the first weeks of life, your newborn may sleep 16 to 18 hours a day, and wake every 45 to 90 minutes. If this feels like something to fix, it isn't. It's biology doing exactly what it should.
What's actually happening
Adult sleep cycles last about 90 minutes and involve long stretches of deep (NREM) sleep. Newborn cycles are roughly half that length and are dominated by active sleep, what we'd call REM. During this phase, the brain is consolidating sensory input, forming neural pathways, and doing the structural work of early development.

"Frequent waking isn't a malfunction. It's the mechanism by which the newborn brain builds itself."

Why it matters for you
Understanding this doesn't make 3am easier. But it can shift the frame: there is nothing wrong with your baby, and there is nothing you failed to do. The sleep will consolidate, typically between 3 and 6 months as NREM sleep gradually increases.
Key points
Newborn sleep cycles are 45–50 min vs. adult 90 min
High REM is protective, not a sign of poor sleep
Consolidation typically begins at 3–6 months
Nothing you're doing is causing the waking
